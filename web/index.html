How a decision is made

Six stages, one direction. Each stage is a separate module you can read in about a minute, and each one writes to the audit log before handing off.

  1. 1

    Intake

    Checks the basics: a name, a positive amount, a term of 12 to 60 months. Bad input stops here with a clear message, and nothing is saved.

    loan_review/intake.py
  2. 2

    Document AI mock

    Reads the uploaded ID, pay stub and bank statement. It pulls out the employer and monthly income for the rules to check.

    loan_review/extraction.py
  3. 3

    Risk score mock

    A 0 to 100 score from four inputs: credit band, debt-to-income, prior late payments and years employed. The formula is published, not hidden.

    loan_review/risk.py
  4. 4

    Lending rules

    Seven rules run in a fixed order, and the first one that fires decides. The result is approve, decline or review, with a one-line reason.

    loan_review/rules.py
  5. 5

    Human review

    Anything uncertain goes to an underwriter. Their name and note are logged next to the automated result, which is kept as it was.

    loan_review/underwriting.py
  6. 6

    Decision and notice

    The decision is recorded and the applicant gets a notice explaining it. The demo stops there, and no money moves.

    loan_review/notify.py

Explainable by design

A decision without a reason is just a guess. Every Meridian decision carries the rule that decided it, the values that rule saw, and a score broken into its parts.

  • The reason is written for the applicant, not for a data scientist.
  • Missing documents are named one by one.
  • All thresholds live in one table, so changing a rule is a single edit.

Humans stay in the loop

The rules handle the clear cases. Borderline scores, income that doesn't match, and missing paperwork all go to an underwriter, who has to write down why they decided.

Their decision is added on top. The automated result underneath is never edited.

Rules engine, 09:14

Every step leaves a record

The application is written once. Each change after that is a new line in an append-only log, with a timestamp, an actor and the details.
